# How trial dimension enters the certificate

This checkpoint distinguishes three different quantities: full Hilbert dimension, variational linear-space dimension, and compressed-state/MPS bond dimension. They cannot be used interchangeably.

For N spin-s dimers, retaining every configuration with at mostq excited dimers gives

 D_q = sum_(k=0)^q binom(N,k)[(2s+1)^2-1]^k.

The exact total-Sz=0 count is the coefficient of z^0 in

 sum_(k=0)^q binom(N,k)[sum_(J=1)^(2s)sum_(m=-J)^J z^m]^k.

For q=2 these simplify to1+N+3binom(N,2) for s=1/2 and1+2N+14binom(N,2) for s=1. At N=16 they are377 and1713. Their full q=4 Sz0 spaces are38877 and1228953. See the exact count JSON for more sizes.

A support generated byp applications of a two-dimer Hamiltonian contains at most2p excited dimers but usually only a small subset of the full shell. For the periodic spin1 chain with N=8 dimers, the actual selected support dimensions are453,5205,43294 at depths2,3,4. The complete H-applied support, not merely the variational space, is included when computing the exact residual.

No universal function of coupling alone bounds the necessary trial dimension. It also depends on size, geometry, spectral isolation, representation, and the required energy or trace accuracy. A growing linear excitation shell can coexist with a constant-bond-dimension product state; the parent's independent binomial product-state toy example makes this distinction exact.

## Certified stopping condition

Let rho be the exact Rayleigh quotient of the rounded trial vector, r2 its exact squared residual, and a a deterministic lower bound for the second eigenvalue. If delta=a-rho>0, then the energy interval width is r2/delta. To target energy width epsilon_E, it is sufficient to verify

 r2 <= delta*epsilon_E.

At inverse temperature beta, reserve an explicit error budget for beta*epsilon_E. For the joint three-axis starting ratios, the relevant denominator powers can be as large as8, so epsilon_E <= tau/(8beta) is a conservative local allocation for logarithmic error budget tau. This is an a posteriori, exact acceptance test. It does not require trusting a fitted convergence rate.

If a<=rho, this one-state Temple certificate does not apply. Improving a vector may help only if its Rayleigh quotient can fall below a. Otherwise improve the no-missed-state bound, certify a larger low band, or change the reference/seed geometry. Small residual alone never resolves this issue.

## Separating failures

- Trial error: beta*r2/delta is not small enough. Increasing selected support or changing the ansatz can help.
- Completeness/tail error: reference bins lie much lower than the actual excitations or count too many states at low energies. More accurate ground digits may have almost no effect. The g=.15 chain depth3/depth4 comparison and the ordinary-bilayer .03 family obstruction exhibit this.
- Physical seed geometry: finite-size ground-energy curvature penalizes spatial purity as beta grows, while thermal purity improves. A temperature may not exist even with exact finite spectra. Increasing n or changing the seed construction is then necessary.

The successful g=.1 chain n=4 result versus failed n=2 proposals illustrates the value of changing the seed length. The failed n=2 proposals alone are not an all-temperature physical exclusion.

All reported coupling frontiers remain method-specific. No claim is made that an unaccepted coupling is gapless, nor that these dimer-based references solve arbitrary nonperturbative models.
