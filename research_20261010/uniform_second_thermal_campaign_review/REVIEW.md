## Blocking resource findings

The proposed maximum6 aggregate CPU hours and at most two one-core256MiB workers are not enforced globally by campaign.py. Its --cpu-budget applies per invocation and resets after restart. Accounting begins after metadata regeneration, so setup CPU is omitted. Failed children raise before a durable CPU record is committed; retries can consume additional unaccounted budget. A case lock does not limit concurrent jobs across different case directories. No wall-clock stop deadline appears in the wrapper. Per-child120CPU-second/256MiB limits are useful but do not imply aggregate/deadline ceilings.

Before launch, a new sealed supervisor must enforce all those limits. A safe design reserves a conservative budget for an entire attempted process tree before launching; uncertain failures retain their debit, while refunds require verified terminal accounting. It must cover metadata/setup, failed attempts and retries; prevent concurrent supervisors from spending the same budget; enforce at most two selected single-core workers and memory limits; and stop all descendants at the agreed deadline. Whole-process-tree accounting or conservative reservations must include child CPU rather than only the Python parent's usage. A stale reservation must not be silently refunded. Deadline checks solely between batches are insufficient if a child can run beyond the deadline. These requirements can be satisfied without changing the frozen data-plane runner.
