## Failure mechanism

The frozen supervisor invokes frozen campaign.py with --cpu-budget120 and --max-new-batches32. The campaign.py runner computes used CPU for its current compute phase and remaining=int(120-used)-1, then sets the NEXT child hard cap=min(120,remaining). It starts the child whenever remaining>=1, without requiring enough allowance for an entire batch. Thus this was a scheduling-boundary failure, not a thermal arithmetic disagreement.

L12I saved 116.495635 childCPU seconds in 16 successful batches, whose durations were 6.806..8.978 seconds. Before the next batch, parent overhead makes remaining<=2 seconds. L12P saved 114.881077 seconds in 12 batches, durations 7.973..11.627 seconds. The next allowance is <=4 seconds. Each next child returned -9, exactly compatible with exhausting this artificially shortened CPU limit. The runner asserted and the watchdog killed its group. The supervisor conservatively retained each full 7320 reservation as UNKNOWN_CHARGED. Committed earlier costs+these reservations gave 16207, so no new 7320 reservation fitted 5393. It stopped normally and did not restart.

The prior fault tests exercised failed children and accounting, but missed this ordinary multi-batch boundary. Any repair must add a scaled nonthermal sequence test where several legal-duration batches approach a short invocation allowance. Correct behavior is successful checkpoint-preserving yield BEFORE launching an underfunded batch, not killing it and charging an unknown whole invocation.
