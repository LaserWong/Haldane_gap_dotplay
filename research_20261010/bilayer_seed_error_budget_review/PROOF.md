## Joint allocation and the 11D Temple obstruction

After reoptimizing only the saved 11D N16 ground vector and 43D N64 numerator, the displayed sufficient width caps are

- g=.105: delta16<=.001201 and delta32<=.000976
- g=.11: delta16<=.001042 and delta32<=.002732

The entire normalized odd and omitted tails remain present. Independent exact ratio enclosures verify all three targets under these caps. Each saved bisection lower-width endpoint succeeds and its upper-width endpoint fails an assigned ratio. Increasing width increases its trace upper and decreases its ratio, so this brackets the threshold for that fixed component allocation. The simple cap is exactly the lower bracket rounded downward to six decimals. The corresponding absolute ground endpoints and existing admissible ground intervals are recomputed exactly.

For a trial with mean R<a, complete second moment B and variance v=B-R^2, the fixed-cutoff Temple value is L_T=R-v/(a-R). For any proposed lower endpoint L,

 (a-R)(L-L_T)=B-(a+L)R+aL.

Thus L_T>L requires the right side to be negative, and L_T>=L requires it to be nonpositive. The compressed pencil Bmat-(a+L)Amat+aL G is independently LDL-positive at both selected N16 targets, with 11 strictly positive pivots. For every nonzero vector in this saved 11D trial space that satisfies R<a, its Temple value is strictly below the selected L. Vectors with R>=a cannot apply this fixed-cutoff formula. Other cutoffs, richer trial spaces, different sufficient allocations and other scalar paths remain outside this obstruction.

Exact finite-dimensional tests separately check 72 scalar cases of the Temple sign identity, and reject negative, zero and later-negative LDL pivots. This guards the inequality direction rather than relying only on a successful factorization.
