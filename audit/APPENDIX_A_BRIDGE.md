## Exact self-adjoint transfer and finite-group sectors

In a real Cartesian basis for spin-1, S_alpha=i A_alpha with A_alpha real antisymmetric. The antiferromagnetic bond obeys -h=sum_alpha A_alpha tensor A_alpha. The nonnegative finite-measure bond-history construction gives a real symmetric Hilbert–Schmidt transfer operator X_beta, including the scalar shift exp(-beta a) per lattice step, with Z_L(beta)=Tr X_beta^L for all integers L>=2. Its spectrum can have either sign. Even powers are positive and trace class.

The tetrahedral rotation group A4 acts unitarily on histories and commutes with X_beta. One concrete action uses the signed Cartesian-coordinate permutations generated by pi rotations and cyclic coordinate permutations. The twisted physical trace equals Tr[U_g X_beta^L]. This follows by performing the spin rotation on the seam in the same local history contraction. The pi and 2pi/3 physical Hamiltonians used for finite computation are conjugate to these group representatives. For 2pi/3, inverse rotations give the same thermal trace by reflection or conjugation.

A4 has irreducibles 1,1',1'',3. The real structure pairs the two complex one-dimensional sectors with identical real X spectra. Decomposing into irreducible representation times multiplicity space therefore gives real eigenvalue lists with integer multiplicities and moments

I_k=(Z_k+3P_k+8C_k)/12,
O_k=(Z_k+3P_k-4C_k)/12,
N_k=(Z_k-P_k)/4,

such that Z_k=I_k+2O_k+3N_k. Here O is one member of the conjugate pair. N is the multiplicity-space moment for the 3-dimensional irrep. These are actual spectral lists, not arbitrary signed moment combinations. For even k all three are nonnegative. This conclusion follows from the finite-group projection operators and Schur's lemma, and remains valid for the compact Hilbert–Schmidt transfer because k>=2 powers are trace class.

The input interval radius 1e-5 also safely bounds every listed moment combination: the absolute coefficient sums are 1, 2/3, and 1/2 for I,O,N.

## Cooling and dominant transfer weight

For any finite physical Hamiltonian, sum_j w_j^r<=(sum_j w_j)^r for r>=1 and nonnegative Boltzmann weights. Cool by r=5/2 to beta*=105/4. Exact comparisons establish

Consequently, at length 30 and beta*, the total transfer moment is at most D. Its trivial-sector moment is at most

tau=(D+11E)/12<=1.225,

and the combined trivial-plus-double-complex moment is at most

tau0=(D+3E)/4<=1.264.

The strict N = 120 trial-state inequality ensures Z120(beta*)>1. Let the normalized-in-length, nonnegative transfer weights be w_i=|lambda_i(beta*)|^30, with the physical multiplicities 1,2,3 included. If every w_i<=.88, then Z120<=.88^3 Z30<=.88^3 D<1, a contradiction. Thus some weight t>.88. Since 2*.88>D, it is unique and lies in the trivial sector. A nontrivial sector would multiply it at least twice. At this stage the sign of lambda is unnecessary because all moments under consideration are even.

With the leading trivial atom removed, let x be remaining trivial mass, y be combined complex-sector mass, and z the triple-sector mass. The constraints are

x>=0,y>=0,x<=tau-t,x+y<=tau0-t,x+y+z<=D-t.

Their squared-weight contribution is at most x^2+y^2/2+z^2/3 because the latter two sectors have equal-copy multiplicities 2 and 3. Replacing z by its upper bound and maximizing the convex quadratic over the polygon gives

R(t)=max [x^2+y^2/2+(D-t-x-y)^2/3]
